## Answers to the 7 Obligatory Questions

### 1. Where is the easiest money?

**ML "reconciled" adjustments ($40.8M).** These are auto-generated system corrections that the platform acknowledges as resolved. If they're resolved, they shouldn't be charged. 80%+ recoverable = **$32.6M** with near-zero operational effort. The system already does it — Eccsa just needs to claim it.

### 2. Which concept has the highest economic impact?

**ML adjustments ($306.6M).** Far larger than any other single lever. A 10% reduction in adjustment ratio adds **$30.7M** to net revenue — more than the entire FALABELLA marketplace and nearly equal to ML's total current net revenue ($32.8M).

### 3. Which negotiation generates the highest return?

**ML logistics rate renegotiation.** $66.5M in gross logistics charges. A 10% reduction = **$6.6M** annual savings. Unlike adjustments (which require behavior change), rate negotiation is a single contract change with one counterparty (Mercado Libre).

### 4. Which marketplace has the best improvement opportunity?

**ML.** Despite being the largest marketplace, it has the lowest net take rate (3.7%), the highest leakage (90.3% adjustment ratio), and the most negotiable concepts. RIPLEY is already optimized (17.9% stable take rate). PARIS is structurally different (1P model).

### 5. Which improvement produces more profit without selling more?

**ML adjustment reduction.** Every $1M in adjustment reduction = $1M directly to bottom line. No need to increase GMV, no operational cost increase. Maximum ROI per unit of effort.

### 6. Which improvement requires the least effort?

**Increase RIPLEY penalidades ($33K → $3.5M).** Currently 0.01% of GMV — industry standard is 1-3%. Implementing a seller compliance penalty program is a simple policy change with measurable impact. However, the absolute impact ($3.5M) is lower than other options.
